Summary – CFO supply plan to Kiunga, 19 Nov 2026 to 13 Jan 2027

Prepared by Myles Rowcliff, OKTL.

The plan keeps Tabubil above one day of use every day and delivers about 38.8 ML to Kiunga over 8 weeks (10.10 ML by river, 28.72 ML by truck), against 19.6 ML of site use.

  • Site use: 350,000 L a day.
  • Trucks: the 7 trucks already loaded (280,000 L) and 4 pre-filled diesel tank-tainers (4 × 21,000 L = 84,000 L, not from Amigo 1) left Aiambak on the morning of 19 Nov; 14 trucks a day (560,000 L) from 20 Nov in three convoys: A at 5 AM (6 trucks), B at 6 AM (4 trucks), C at 7 AM (4 trucks). Convoys leave on time; any truck that can't be fuelled waits at Aiambak for the next convoy.
  • Fly Challenger – one-off run to Kiunga: left Aiambak at 8 AM 19 Nov with 1.2 ML, due at Kiunga about 8 AM 22 Nov. After 12 hours discharging it returns to Aiambak (25 Nov) and on to POM (about 8 PM 29 Nov), then goes back to normal POM – Aiambak trips.
  • Fly Resilience: alongside at Aiambak with 1.5 ML but not discharging yet. The plan assumes it starts topping up Amigo 1 at 6 AM 20 Nov (to be confirmed) and leaves Aiambak about 8 AM 21 Nov, then normal trips.
  • Amigo 1: 1,650,000 L on the evening of 19 Nov (capacity 2,100,000 L). 12 trucks (480,000 L) are being filled from it now for the 20 Nov convoys, leaving 1,170,000 L.
  • Fly vessels: Challenger, Resilience and Prosperity at 1.5 ML from their next loading, Warrior 1.0 ML; Warrior starts loading at POM on 21 Nov (taken as midday) and reaches Aiambak about midday 26 Nov.
  • Diamond: POM – Aiambak shuttle (1 day loading, 4 days each way, 12 hours discharging). Its first trip reaches Aiambak on 23 Nov and refuels Elimbari; after that it refuels Elimbari when it is waiting, otherwise it discharges into Amigo 1.
  • Elimbari: the only vessel on Aiambak – Kiunga, about one full run (950,000 – 1,000,000 L) a week. It gets first call on Fly vessel and Diamond CFO at Aiambak.
  • Wonderland: POM – Aiambak shuttle into Amigo 1 from 5 Dec, 9-day cycle with 1.5 days at each end (1 day freight, 12 hours CFO).
  • Site stock: lowest about 700,000 L (2 days of use) around midday 19 Nov, before the first trucks and tank-tainers arrive. Elimbari discharges 1.0 ML at Kiunga from about 7 AM 20 Nov and Challenger 1.2 ML from 22 Nov. From 20 Nov end-of-day site stock stays above 2.1 ML.

Trucks held at Aiambak

Convoys leave on time with every truck Amigo 1 can fill. A truck that can't be fuelled waits at Aiambak and goes with the next convoy once there is CFO. With Challenger's 1.2 ML going straight to Kiunga instead of into Amigo 1, this happens on a few days:

  • 25 Nov: 8 trucks (320,000 L); 26 Nov: no trucks until Fly Warrior arrives.
  • 2 Dec: 5 trucks (200,000 L).
  • 4 Dec: 10 trucks (400,000 L).
  • 7 Dec: 10 trucks (400,000 L).
  • 14 Dec: 8 trucks (320,000 L).
  • 26 Dec: 12 trucks (480,000 L).

Site stock is at least 4.1 ML on each of those days, so they have no effect on Tabubil.

Risks and open items

  • Fly Resilience discharge: if it hasn't started by the 22 Nov convoys, Amigo 1 runs out that morning (about 13 trucks) and no trucks leave 23 – 26 Nov until Fly Warrior arrives. Site stock would still stay above 2.6 ML.
  • Road: if the road closes, Kiunga relies on Elimbari alone, about 1 ML a week – well under site use over a long closure.
  • Fly vessel waiting: about 79 hours in total over 8 weeks while Amigo 1 is full.
How the schedule is worked out

POM–Aiambak round trip 9 days (including a day alongside at Aiambak) plus 1 day loading at POM; a Fly vessel does not leave Aiambak until all its CFO is off, so it waits when Amigo 1 is full. Aiambak–Kiunga round trip 5 days plus 12 hours refuelling at Aiambak and 12 hours discharging at Kiunga. River vessels get first call on Fly vessel CFO at Aiambak; whatever is left goes into Amigo 1, which trucks out in three convoys a day. Wonderland shuttles POM–Aiambak on a 9-day cycle into Amigo 1. Diamond (950,000 L) shuttles POM–Aiambak like a Fly vessel. The schedule is simulated hour by hour. Lead time counts only CFO that goes to Kiunga by river.
